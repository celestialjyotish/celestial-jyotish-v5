# Celestial Jyotish V5 — Visual Bible

## 1. PURPOSE

The Visual Bible defines the permanent visual language of Celestial Jyotish V5.

The objective is to make every generated image, scene, thumbnail and cinematic sequence feel like part of the same universe.

The visual identity must communicate:

- premium production quality
- cinematic realism
- Indian cultural context
- emotional realism
- visual storytelling
- astrological depth
- character continuity

The system should produce imagery that feels like a frame from a premium cinematic production rather than generic AI artwork.

---

## 2. MASTER VISUAL IDENTITY

Default visual direction:

- Hollywood cinematic realism
- Epic Realism
- photorealistic
- premium streaming-series quality
- sophisticated cinematic lighting
- realistic materials
- realistic skin
- natural anatomy
- believable environments
- controlled depth of field
- strong composition
- emotionally meaningful framing

When appropriate, the visual language may use:

- ARRI Alexa-style cinematic rendering
- large-format cinematic depth
- premium filmic color treatment
- realistic lens behavior
- atmospheric depth
- controlled highlights and shadows

These descriptors should support the image rather than become meaningless prompt decoration.

---

## 3. REALISM STANDARD

Characters must look physically believable.

Prioritize:

- realistic skin texture
- realistic hair
- realistic hands
- realistic eyes
- natural facial anatomy
- believable body proportions
- realistic clothing folds
- physically plausible lighting
- believable reflections
- realistic materials
- natural environmental detail

Avoid:

- plastic skin
- wax-like faces
- exaggerated anatomy
- malformed hands
- impossible body proportions
- artificial facial symmetry
- excessive beauty-filter appearance
- generic stock-photo expressions

---

## 4. CINEMATIC PRIORITY

When a scene contains:

CHARACTER + ACTION + ENVIRONMENT

the visual hierarchy should normally be:

1. Character
2. Meaningful action
3. Emotional expression
4. Important object
5. Environment
6. Atmospheric detail

Do not allow decorative cosmic effects to overpower the human story.

---

## 5. CHARACTER CONTINUITY

Dev and Asha are governed by `CHARACTER_BIBLE.yaml`.

The image system must preserve:

- face
- body type
- eye color
- hair
- skin tone
- age
- ethnicity
- fundamental emotional identity

Wardrobe may change.

Environment may change.

Camera angle may change.

Lighting may change.

Story situation may change.

Character identity must not drift.

---

## 6. VISUAL STORYTELLING

Whenever possible, communicate meaning through visible action.

Prefer:

A character repairing something

over:

A character explaining the concept of repair.

Prefer:

A character stopping before reacting

over:

A narrator saying "control your anger."

Prefer:

Two characters rebuilding something together

over:

A dialogue explaining reconciliation.

The visual should carry part of the narrative burden.

---

## 7. CAMERA LANGUAGE

Camera choice must have narrative purpose.

### ECU — Extreme Close-Up

Use for:

- eyes
- hands
- important objects
- subtle emotional detail
- critical symbolic moments

### CU — Close-Up

Use for:

- emotional reactions
- realization
- restrained anger
- intimate moments

### MCU — Medium Close-Up

Use for:

- character interaction
- dialogue
- controlled emotional scenes

### MS — Medium Shot

Use for:

- physical actions
- work
- movement
- interaction with important objects

### MWS — Medium Wide Shot

Use for:

- character plus environment
- relationship dynamics
- physical tasks

### WS — Wide Shot

Use for:

- establishing environment
- isolation
- scale
- movement through space

### EWS — Extreme Wide Shot

Use selectively for:

- geographic scale
- major environmental contrast
- cinematic transition

Do not use wide shots merely because they look impressive.

---

## 8. CAMERA MOVEMENT

Camera movement should support the emotional state.

Possible movements:

### Slow push-in

Use for:

- realization
- emotional discovery
- growing tension
- revelation

### Controlled tracking

Use for:

- movement through an environment
- pursuit
- transition
- purposeful walking

### Slow lateral movement

Use for:

- observation
- relationship dynamics
- revealing information

### Static frame

Use for:

- restraint
- emotional weight
- uncomfortable silence
- important decisions

### Handheld movement

Use selectively for:

- instability
- urgency
- conflict
- heightened physical tension

Do not make every scene move.

---

## 9. LENS LANGUAGE

Lens selection should serve the scene.

### Wide-angle

Useful for:

- environment
- spatial relationships
- dynamic movement
- architectural scale

### Normal perspective

Useful for:

- natural human interaction
- everyday realism
- dialogue

### Telephoto

Useful for:

- emotional isolation
- compressed environments
- observation
- intimate moments from distance

Avoid excessive artificial lens effects.

---

## 10. LIGHTING

Lighting should be motivated by the environment whenever possible.

Preferred sources:

- window light
- practical lamps
- warm interior lighting
- street lighting
- sunlight
- moonlight
- workplace lighting
- realistic architectural lighting

Use cinematic lighting to enhance meaning, not to make every frame look artificially dramatic.

---

## 11. COLOR LANGUAGE

The palette should remain premium and controlled.

Preferred overall direction:

- deep charcoal
- dark brown
- warm ivory
- muted gold
- emerald
- deep jewel tones
- realistic skin tones
- controlled highlights

Color should communicate emotional or thematic change.

Examples:

Warmth
→ connection / safety / intimacy

Cooler tones
→ distance / uncertainty / isolation

Gold
→ significance / knowledge / revelation

Emerald
→ Asha's visual identity and selected celestial themes

Red / heat
→ urgency / conflict / Mars-related visual moments

These associations are creative tools, not rigid rules.

---

## 12. INDIAN CONTEXT

Indian identity should feel authentic rather than decorative.

Use believable environments such as:

- modern Indian homes
- apartments
- offices
- streets
- cafes
- studios
- family spaces
- workplaces
- temples where genuinely relevant
- traditional interiors when appropriate
- contemporary urban environments

Indian clothing should be culturally believable.

Avoid unnecessary exoticization.

Do not insert temples, diyas, mandalas or zodiac symbols into every astrology scene.

---

## 13. ASTROLOGICAL VISUALIZATION

Astrology may be visualized directly when the topic requires it.

Possible elements:

- Kundali charts
- planetary diagrams
- house layouts
- Nakshatra imagery
- celestial bodies
- degrees
- technical annotations
- classical manuscripts
- astronomical imagery

However, technical graphics should be used when they improve understanding.

Do not place random zodiac wheels or glowing planets in the background simply to signal "astrology."

## 18. COMPOSITION

The composition should establish clear visual hierarchy.

Prioritize:

- subject placement
- eye-line
- negative space
- foreground/background separation
- depth
- object placement
- environmental context

Do not overcrowd the frame.

The viewer should immediately understand what matters.

---

## 19. DEPTH

Whenever appropriate, use:

FOREGROUND
→ SUBJECT
→ BACKGROUND

to create cinematic depth.

Useful elements include:

- doorways
- furniture
- architectural frames
- windows
- foreground objects
- atmospheric layers

Depth should remain realistic.

---

## 20. THUMBNAIL LANGUAGE

YouTube thumbnails and social thumbnails require stronger visual compression than cinematic scenes.

Prioritize:

- one dominant subject
- one emotional state
- clear silhouette
- readable face
- strong visual hierarchy
- limited text
- strong contrast
- recognizable topic

The thumbnail must remain readable at small size.

Do not fill the frame with unnecessary astrology symbols.

---

## 21. 9:16 VERTICAL COMPOSITION

For Shorts/Reels:

- protect important face and action from UI cropping
- keep primary subject inside the central safe area
- avoid placing critical text at extreme top/bottom
- compose vertically rather than simply cropping a horizontal scene
- use depth to exploit vertical space

---

## 22. 16:9 COMPOSITION

For YouTube long-form:

- use horizontal cinematic composition
- allow environmental storytelling
- maintain clear subject hierarchy
- reserve appropriate negative space for titles when needed
- avoid overly centered composition unless intentional

---

## 23. TEXT IN GENERATED IMAGES

Do not depend on image-generation models for precise typography unless the model is known to handle it reliably.

Prefer:

IMAGE GENERATION
→ TEXT / GRAPHICS IN POST-PRODUCTION

When text is necessary inside an image:

- keep it short
- verify spelling
- verify language
- verify placement
- verify readability

---

## 24. IMAGE PROMPT STRUCTURE

A production-ready image prompt should normally establish:

1. Format / aspect ratio
2. Subject
3. Character lock
4. Physical action
5. Environment
6. Composition
7. Camera framing
8. Lens perspective
9. Lighting
10. Emotional state
11. Important props
12. Visual symbolism
13. Material realism
14. Cinematic finish
15. Continuity requirements

Do not merely stack quality adjectives.

Describe the actual image.

---

## 25. VIDEO PROMPT STRUCTURE

A video-generation prompt should normally specify:

1. Starting visual state
2. Character identity
3. Physical action
4. Character expression
5. Camera movement
6. Environmental movement
7. Lighting continuity
8. Timing
9. Sound/SFX direction where supported
10. End state
11. Continuity constraints

Prefer one major physical action per short generation.

---

## 27. CINEMATIC CONTINUITY

When multiple shots depict the same scene, maintain:

- time of day
- lighting direction
- wardrobe
- hairstyle
- props
- character position
- environmental details
- emotional state
- physical continuity

A continuation should feel like the next shot of the same production.

---

## 28. IMAGE-TO-VIDEO CONTINUITY

When an image becomes the seed for video:

Do not introduce major new objects unless explicitly required.

Do not change:

- face
- body
- clothing
- environment
- lighting direction

unless the transition is intentional.

Animate what already exists.

---

## 29. VISUAL ATTENTION

Use visual attention deliberately.

For important moments consider:

- closer framing
- stronger contrast
- movement
- facial reaction
- object reveal
- visual interruption
- environmental change

Do not make every shot maximally intense.

Visual hierarchy creates attention.

---

## 30. FINAL VISUAL TEST

Before approving a visual, ask:

Is the main subject immediately clear?

Is the physical action understandable?

Does the image communicate something about the story?

Are Dev/Asha consistent?

Does the environment feel believable?

Does the Indian context feel authentic where relevant?

Is the lighting motivated?

Is the composition cinematic?

Is the astrology integrated meaningfully?

Does it avoid generic AI aesthetics?

Would this frame belong in the same Celestial Jyotish universe as the previous episode?

If not, revise before production.
